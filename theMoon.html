What is Our Moon?

Our moon has a radius of about 1,080 miles (1,740 kilometers), which is less than a third of the Earth's width. The moon has a very thin and weak atmosphere and does not provide any protection from the Sun's radiation. It's surface has a temperature that reaches about 260 degrees Fahrenheit (127 degrees Celsius) when in full Sun, but in darkness, the temperatures plummet to about -280 degrees Fahrenheit (-173 degrees Celsius) The Moon has a core, mantle, and crust. Its core, much like Earth has a solid and a liquid core. The solid part is 149 miles (240 kilometers) in radius and the liquid part, which surrounds the solid part, is about 56 miles (90 kilometers) thick.

Water In The Moon

The first discovery of water on the Moon was made in 2008 by the Indian mission Chandrayaan-1. It detected hydroxyl molecules spread across the lunar surface and concentrated at the poles. In October 2020, NASA's Stratospheric Observatory for Infrared Astronomy (SOFIA) confirmed, for the first time, water on the sunlit surface of the Moon.
