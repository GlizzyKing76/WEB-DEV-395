What is Jupiter?

Jupiter is the biggest planet in our solar system, it has a radius of 43,440.7 miles (69,911 kilometers) and is about 11 times wider than Earth. It is a gas giant, which means it is mostly made of hydrogen and helium. Jupiter's composition is similar to that of the Sun, which is why it is sometimes referred to as a "failed star." This also gives Jupiter the largest ocean in our Solar System, only that it is not made of water, but of liquid metallic hydrogen. A spacecraft would not be able to land on Jupiter, it would just sink into the planet's atmosphere and eventually be crushed by the intense pressure.

Jupiter is known for its Great Red Spot, which is a giant storm that has been raging for at least 400 years. The storm is so big that it could fit three Earths inside it. The Great Red Spot is a high-pressure region in Jupiter's atmosphere, and it is believed to be a result of the planet's fast rotation and the complex interactions between its atmosphere and magnetic field.
