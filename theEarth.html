Welcome to Earth

What is Earth?

Earth is the biggest terrestrial planet in our solar system and the fifth largest planet overall. It has an average distance of 93 million miles (150 million kilometers) from the Sun and is the only planet known to support life. Earth orbits the sun once every 365.25 days, which is why we have a leap year every four years to account for the extra quarter of a day, it also completes one rotation every 23.9 hours. Earth has an axis of 23.4 degrees, which is responsible for our yearly seasons.

During part of the year, the Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun, while the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away from it. This causes the Northern Hemisphere to experience summer while the Southern Hemisphere experiences winter, and vice versa. The Earth is composed of three main layers: the crust, the mantle, and the core. The crust is the outermost layer, which is made up of solid rock and is where we live. The earth's core is made up of two parts: the outer core, which is liquid, and the inner core, which is solid. The core is responsible for generating Earth's magnetic field, which protects us from harmful solar radiation.

Our Moon

The moon is Earth's only natural satellite and is the fifth largest moon in the solar system. It has a diameter of 2,159 miles (3,474 kilometers) and is about 1/4 the size of Earth. The moon is about 238,855 miles (384,400 kilometers) away from Earth and takes about 27.3 days to complete one orbit around our planet. Some moons are bits of rocks that were captured by a planet's gravity, while others are thought to have formed from the same material as their parent planet. But our moon is likely the result of a collision between Earth and a Mars-sized object called Theia, which occurred about 4.5 billion years ago.
