What is Earth?

Earth is the biggest terrestrial planet in our solar system and the fifth largest planet overall. It has an average distance of 93 million miles (150 million kilometers) from the Sun and is the only planet known to support life. Earth orbits the sun once every 365.25 days, which is why we have a leap year every four years to account for the extra quarter of a day, it also completes one rotation every 23.9 hours. Earth has an axis of 23.4 degrees, which is responsible for our yearly seasons. The Earth is composed of three main layers: the crust, the mantle, and the core. The crust is the outermost layer, which is made up of solid rock and is where we live. The earth's core is made up of two parts: the outer core, which is liquid, and the inner core, which is solid. The core is responsible for generating Earth's magnetic field, which protects us from harmful solar radiation.

Earth's Surface

Earth, like Mars and Venus has volcanoes, mountains, and valleys. Earth's global ocean covers about 71% of the planet's surface, it has an average depth of 2.3 miles (3.6 kilometers) and contains 97% of Earth's water.
