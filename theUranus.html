What is Uranus?

Uranus was the first planet discovered with a telescope, and was discovered by William Herschel in 1781. It is named after the Greek god of the sky, Uranus. Uranus is an ice giant, it is surrounded by 13 known rings, and has 28 small moons. It is the seventh planet form the Sun, and is the third largest planet in our solar system. It rotates at a nearly 90-degree angle, which means it rotates on its side. Uranus is the coldest planet in our solar system, with an average temperature of -224 degrees Celsius.

Uranus has an equatorial diameter of 51,118 kilometers (31,763 miles), it is four times the diameter of Earth. It has an average distance of 1.8 billion miles (2.9 billion kilometers) from the Sun, and takes 84 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun. Uranus has a very thin atmosphere, which is made up of hydrogen, helium, and methane. The methane in the atmosphere gives Uranus its blue color.
