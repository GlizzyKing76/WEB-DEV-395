What is Uranus?

Uranus was the first planet discovered with a telescope, and was discovered by William Herschel in 1781. It is named after the Greek god of the sky, Uranus. Uranus is an ice giant, it is surrounded by 13 known rings, and has 28 small moons. Uranus has an equatorial diameter of 51,118 kilometers (31,763 miles), it is four times the diameter of Earth. It has an average distance of 1.8 billion miles (2.9 billion kilometers) from the Sun, and takes 84 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun. Uranus has a very thin atmosphere, which is made up of hydrogen, helium, and methane. The methane in the atmosphere gives Uranus its blue color.

Uranus has 28 moons that we know of, all of it's inner moons are roughly half water ice and half rock. We still don't know the composition of the outer moons however, it is believed that they are likely captured asteroids.
